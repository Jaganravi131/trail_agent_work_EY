# Integrity & Compliance — the rules that keep this asset alive

This file exists because most zero-capital content operations don't fail at the idea stage. They fail by getting an account suspended, a book removed, or a reader harmed. Three ways to lose everything you've built, and the rule for each.

## 1. Author identity and credentials

- **The bio as written is true:** lived experience around overnight work + public guidance, translated into a procedure. It explicitly says the author is **not a clinician.**
- **Never** add "RN", "BSN", "board-certified", "sleep specialist", "RD", "PhD", or a white coat to the cover, bio, author page or any post. A fabricated credential on a health-adjacent title is (a) consumer fraud, (b) an immediate KDP takedown trigger, (c) the thing that hurts someone who trusts it instead of their doctor.
- A pen name is legitimate. A fake résumé is not. If you have real clinical or occupational-health experience, say so precisely, and be ready to substantiate it if a reviewer asks.
- If you want authority without the fraud, the free path is what the book already does: cite named primary sources in-text, and link the actual guidance.

## 2. Health claims

Allowed: "Protein at the first hour reduces hunger at 02:00 for most people." "Caffeine's half-life is commonly given around 5 hours." "Occupational guidance favours forward rotation and capping consecutive nights."
Not allowed anywhere:
- Any claim to **treat, cure or prevent** a disease (including "fixes insomnia", "heals your circadian rhythm", "prevents diabetes").
- Supplement or drug dosing as a recommendation. The book mentions melatonin only as "ask your clinician about timing" and never a dose. Keep it that way in any derivative post.
- Anything that reads as individual medical advice, or "consult a professional" as a fig leaf while still telling people what to do.
- Undermining workplace safety rules, or advising a fatigue-compounded person to drive. Chapter 6's absolute no-drowsy-driving rule is not decorative and must appear in every derivative article and post.

**Also:** the tool's `.ics` alarm for "wake — fixed anchor" must never be positioned as treatment for a sleep disorder, and the footer disclaimer must stay on the page.

## 3. AI disclosure on Amazon

KDP asks at upload whether content is AI-**generated** or AI-**assisted**. Answer factually for the actual workflow:
- Human reviewed, fact-checked, edited and accountable for every claim → "AI-assisted" (with your own verification of the six claims in the roadmap).
- Manuscript shipped as-is without substantive review → that's "AI-generated", and you should not publish it in that state on a topic touching health. You are the publisher; the responsibility is not deferrable to a model.
Falsely declaring it is a policy violation with permanent account consequences. Amazon has also removed bulk low-quality AI titles; the moat here is specificity and worksheets, and you protect it by keeping the specificity.

## 4. Affiliate links, reviews and FTC/ASA rules

- Any affiliate link gets a **plain-language disclosure on the same page, above the fold, before the link.** "Some links on this page are affiliate links; if you buy through them I may earn a commission at no cost to you." Put it in the article front-matter position, not the footer at pixel 9,000.
- Amazon Associates requires the specific statement in their Operating Agreement, including the "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" wording, on the site and in any email that contains Associates links. Read the current agreement; it changes, and violating it loses the account and the accrued commissions.
- **Never**: buy reviews, swap reviews, have colleagues post reviews as "readers", offer a refund for a positive review, or post in a community without disclosing you made the thing. Ask for honest reviews only, in person, one at a time, and say "only if it actually helped."
- No incentivised review schemes, no free copies conditional on a review, no review-gating (showing happy customers a review link and angry ones a support form).

## 5. Scraping, copyright and "transformative"

- No scraping of Amazon reviews, Goodreads, Reddit private content, or competitor listings for keywords. Demand signals here come from autocomplete-style reasoning + your own knowledge, and you hand-verify 5 phrases in an incognito window. That's legal and it's enough.
- No text, figures or tables copied from clinical guidelines, textbooks, or other books. Facts and figures aren't copyrightable; *sentences* are. Every paragraph here is original composition, and any edit must stay that way.
- No images you can't verify the licence of. The cover uses zero photography for this reason. Use your own photos, or genuinely free-commercial-license sources (Pexels/Unsplash licences permit commercial print — but read the current terms at download time and keep the receipt in the repo).
- No trademarks in title, subtitle, keywords or cover: not "Casper", "Sleep Number", "Nespresso", "Huggies", no "Keurig-compatible", no clinician or celebrity names as keyword bait. Generic nouns ("a 3D-cup eye mask") only.

## 6. Platform terms, and the ones people break first

| Platform | The rule that gets accounts killed | This build's posture |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit | Undisclosed self-promo, vote manipulation, reposting after a removal | Value-first, answer the question, link on request only, respect the moderator's removal |
| Amazon KDP | Misdeclared AI content, bulk near-identical titles, keyword stuffing in the title | One specific book, 7 keywords, no brand terms, honest declaration |
| Medium | Automated/mass-generated publishing without real substance | Manual, one article at a time, no syndication spam |
| Substack | Cold lists, purchased addresses | Opt-in only, one email/week |
| Google | Scaled content abuse, doorway pages | 12 pages maximum, each answering a real question |
| Etsy/Gumroad | Reselling template packs copied from others | 100% original worksheets, described accurately |
| GitHub Pages | Using it as a file host for pirated material | Static site, MIT-licensed tool code, no binaries beyond the cover |

## 7. Reader-safety floor (non-negotiable, applies to every asset)

Someone reading this at 07:40 is sleep-deprived and may be about to drive. Every asset in this folder therefore keeps:
1. A visible "not medical advice" line.
2. An explicit "never drive drowsy" rule, with the safe alternative.
3. A "see a clinician if…" list, not a hedge but a real redirect.
4. Nothing that asks a reader to change a safety-critical work practice against their employer's protocol.

If a future article or post drops any of the four, delete the post before it does harm — reputationally and, potentially, literally.

## 8. Legal posture

Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice. If you publish under a business name in the US, a sole proprietorship is the free default; register nothing until revenue is consistent, then ask a professional in your jurisdiction. If your *employer* forbids moonlighting or side income, read your contract before publishing anything under a semi-pseudonymous byline on a topic adjacent to your job — the risk isn't legal, it's HR.
