# Iteration Playbook — diagnosis before new work

When numbers don't move, the instinct is to make another thing. That's how people end up with four unpublished assets and no information. Diagnose first.

## The tree: start at the top and stop at the first match

**"Nobody sees it"** (impressions < 200/wk anywhere by day 30)
- Cover unreadable at 100 px? Screenshot a real Amazon result page, shrink it, look. If the title is illegible, it's packaging, not product. → New cover, same everything else. Re-test 14 days.
- Subtitle has no searchable nouns? "Nurses, EMTs, warehouse" must be *in* the subtitle. → Rewrite from `02-kdp/02-KDP-BACKEND-UPLOAD.md`, re-publish, 72 h.
- Zero external traffic? You didn't ship 30 days of posts. The calendar is the acquisition channel; the book is only the conversion. → Ship 10 posts this week before touching the book.
- Site: not in Search Console, or no sitemap submitted? Fix before writing anything.

**"Seen, not clicked"** (store views or GSC impressions fine, CTR < 0.5%)
- Title/trust. Do you have <5 reviews? No reviews is a conversion ceiling, not a ranking problem: **10 personal asks today**, not a "please review" post.
- Price above $4.99 with no reviews? → $2.99 for 10 days as a stated "review week".
- Search snippet mismatch (title promises A, query asked B) → rewrite H1 + first 100 words, not the whole article.

**"Clicked, not bought"** (page views, <1% buy)
- Description first 2 lines carry the mechanism? Most readers never expand. Lead with "the 90-minute sequence" and "30 g of protein at the anchor meal" — specifics, not adjectives.
- Is there a table of contents visible? Add it to the Look Inside sample. Sample quality drives more conversions than price in this band.
- Categories too competitive? Move to the thinner shelf, request the third category.
- Reviews exist but are 3★ with "too short / wanted more"? That's a real signal: expand ch. 3–5 with the worksheets inline, re-upload, and reply to that review with "added in v2 — thanks."
- 1★ saying "generic AI content"? Read it, accept it, fix it. Do not argue. Non-response is fine; defensiveness in a review reply is a permanent public record.

**"Bought, not read"** (KENP stalls, sales but no full reads)
- Chapter 1 doesn't deliver a usable number on page 1 → move the five-numbers table earlier.
- Middle sags → insert a second worksheet, not more prose.

**"Read, no repeat purchase / no list growth"**
- No back-matter CTA to the newsletter. It exists in `01-book/chapters/10-back-matter.md` — check it points at a live URL.
- No email prompt on the tool or articles. This is the single cheapest fix in the stack, and the one most people never do.

**"Everything fine but no money"**
- Volume ceiling, correctly diagnosed. Then it's an *assortment* problem: book 2, the $19 bundle, and the $9 kit at a price that converts. Not a re-do of book 1.

## What usually fails (learned from the pattern, not luck)

| Failure | Frequency | Root cause |
|---|---|---|
| Publishing with no cover test | very common | 60–80% of the outcome, done in 10 minutes of looking at it small |
| <10 reviews at day 60 | most common | not asking individually |
| Quitting at day 34 | extremely common | no tracker, so a normal ramp looked like failure |
| Scaling to 40 AI articles | fatal | 2026 search enforcement; kills the domain for the useful 3 |
| Vague health claims creeping into posts | fatal | a moderator screenshot is forever, and it can hurt someone |
| Adding analytics instead of adding distribution | very common | feels like progress, is procrastination |
| New project before asset 1 has 30 days | near-universal | you learn nothing transferable; the compounding never starts |

## Test protocol

One variable per 14 days. Price, cover, subtitle, description — one at a time, with the tracker row explaining which. Change two and you've produced noise, at cost.

## Stop conditions (decide them now, while it's neutral)

- **Day 60, <15 units and <10 reviews, and you shipped the calendar** → packaging failure. One full cover + subtitle redo, then 30 more days. Still nothing at day 100: keep the site and tool, retire the book from ads-free promotion, and move the framework to a niche where the buyer is a professional (see below). Do not abandon the *machine*.
- **Day 60 with 500+ tool visits and no sales** → the audience is real and the offer is wrong. That's the good failure mode: ship the $9 kit as the primary product, book as the lead magnet.
- **Weeks where you can't ship 3 assets** → shrink to 1/week rather than stopping. Compounding is a function of not stopping, not of intensity.
- **Any health-related review that suggests harm** ("did this make you fall asleep at the wheel?") → pause all promotion, edit the manuscript, add the safety warning where it belongs, then resume. Immediately, without waiting for a pattern.

## Pivot ladder (same machine, next niche)

Ranked by how much of the existing work carries over:

1. ***The Days Off Problem*** — book 2, same reader, same cover system, 60% content reuse of the framework.
2. **Nights for carers/home health aides** — same physiology, different vocabulary, near-zero book supply. Re-record the examples; the skeleton is done.
3. **Emergency dispatchers / overnight IT & security operations** — higher willingness to pay, $19–29 kit possible, professional association newsletters as free distribution.
4. **Long-haul drivers** — huge population, real safety angle, union/trade press as distribution. Requires a fatigue-and-driving section written with more care than anything in this book.
5. **Students in clinical rotations** — cheap paperbacks, bulk sales to programmes, low review volume.

The reusable parts in every pivot: the build pipeline, the KDP account with reviews, the author page, the tool code (change copy, not structure), the tracking sheet, the diagnosis tree. That's what "compounding" means concretely — and it's why the second asset costs about a fifth of the first.
