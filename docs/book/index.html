The Night Shift Reset

A 28-day system for sleeping on the wrong hours. Nine chapters, a worksheet pack, front and back matter. About 11,600 words, which is roughly 46 pages at 6×9 — deliberately short, because a shift worker will not read 250 pages at 13:00 with an alarm set.

If you want to spend nothing: the planner does the timing math this book explains, in a browser, for free. Plenty of people need only that. This book is for the ones who need the why — because the why is what makes the plan survive a bad roster, a new flat, or a partner who doesn't work nights.

What's inside

  1. The Clock You Can't See — light hitting the wrist, the body's own night-time signal, why "just go to bed earlier" fails, and what a fixed wake time actually does. The mechanism, in four pages, no jargon.
  2. The Descent — the three hours before you clock in. The 3-2-1-0 countdown, the anchor meal, why the last caffeine lands where it does, and how to fall asleep at 13:00 without lying there doing arithmetic.
  3. The Hold — eating between midnight and six. What to actually bring, one microwave and a 20-minute break, no fridge at all, and when the right move is to drink water and sit down.
  4. Caffeine Is a Scheduler, Not a Starter — the 400 mg/day ceiling, 200 mg a dose, the ~5-hour half-life, and the curfew that makes the rest of the plan work.
  5. The Light Curtain and the $0 Bedroom Rebuild — the four changes that matter, in order of what they cost you. Most of them cost nothing.
  6. The Reentry — the ninety minutes after you clock out: sunglasses, the drive home, and why the phone in your hand at 08:00 is the reason 13:00 felt impossible.
  7. Days Off, the Bridge Day, and Getting a Better Roster — forward versus backward rotation, the one-day pivot, and how to ask for a schedule change with a specific, winnable request.
  8. The 28-Day Protocol — the whole thing as a calendar you mark up, with what to change if week two goes badly.
  9. Worksheets — the log, the sleep-fit check, the roster conversation sheet, and the fridge card you print and stick inside a cupboard door.

Who should not buy it

  • You're on nights for a fortnight and then done. Don't. Read chapter 2 and use the planner. A book you'll finish in one shift is not the right purchase.
  • You cannot sleep at all, in any setup, at any time, for weeks. That's not a hygiene problem, and this book is not the treatment: see a clinician about it first. The editorial policy says why this site refuses to pretend otherwise.
  • You want a supplement protocol. This book contains none, on purpose. It is the single most common thing night-shift books get wrong and the reason most of them get returned.

What this book is not

It is not medical advice and not a treatment for shift work sleep disorder or anything else. The author is not a nurse, a doctor, a sleep technologist, or a coach with a certificate — that's stated in the front matter of the book itself, not hidden on a website. It gives no supplement protocol and no dose of anything; if you're already taking something for sleep, that's a pharmacist conversation, not a book's. Written by someone who did the shifts, kept the log, and then read the primary studies so you don't have to. The drowsy-driving warning is in chapter 2, not in the fine print: if you are that tired, drive home first and sleep after, and if you're nodding off at the wheel, the shift is the lesser problem.

Every claim about what research shows is written so a reader can check it: the citation is in the back matter, and the site's rule is soften-or-cut when a number can't be verified. That includes the numbers above. If a figure on this page disagrees with the store page, the store page is right and this one gets corrected — corrections are dated and listed, not quietly edited.

Why the free tool is on the same site as the paid book

Because the tool answers the question "what time do I do each thing," and it does it for your specific shift start, which a PDF can't. The book answers "why this and not that," plus the parts that don't fit in a calculator: the roster conversation, the days off, the partner who's awake, the flat where the bedroom faces east. If the planner alone fixes your week, keep the $0 and be glad.
