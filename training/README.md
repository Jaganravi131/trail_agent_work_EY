## Do not skip

1. **Licence before benchmark.** The corpus is gate-passed work the operator owns, so training on it is clean. Do not add outputs scraped from models whose terms forbid using their outputs to train other models (the Llama 4-family licence and several 'modified MIT' releases do exactly that) — `wf model --select` filters those out for this reason, not as a preference.
2. **Eval the adapter, don't admire it.** `wf eval` measures constraint obedience and specificity. Fixing prose style while quietly breaking instruction-following is a net loss, and only the battery shows it.
3. **Small corpus = small claims.** This tunes voice and format. It cannot add facts, and it will faithfully amplify whatever tic appears thirty times in the training data.

## What the dataset builder recommends right now

- fine_tune: **not yet** — 117 SFT examples and 0 preference pairs is below the floor (150/40). A LoRA on this would mostly learn to imitate formatting while degrading instruction-following, and you would have no way to tell the difference at 40 examples.
  - instead: Ship the constraint prompt as-is and use few-shot examples from this same corpus — the marginal gain from a LoRA at this data volume is within noise.
  - instead: Keep writing. Every approved asset adds examples; the corpus is the scarce input, not the training run.
  - instead: Spend the time on the gate + eval harness, which improves output quality immediately and keeps improving as models change.
