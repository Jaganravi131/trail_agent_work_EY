# Days off after nights

![A printed eight-day roster on a fridge with the bridge day circled in pencil — replace with a real photo of your own setup](images/bridge-day-calendar.jpg)

The days off are where night-shift sleep is won or lost, and almost nobody treats them as part of the schedule. They get treated as the reward: the first off day is spent recovering on the sofa, the second is the one you'd been looking forward to all week, and by the third you're awake at 02:00 on your own day off, wired, with a shift starting tomorrow. Then the advice you get is "enjoy your days off properly", which is a sentence from someone who doesn't have to work the Thursday.

There are only two sane strategies, and the choice between them is not a preference. It's arithmetic about how many off days you have and what you want to do with them.

| Strategy | What it is | Costs you | Buys you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hold** | Stay on night time the whole block. Sleep in the day, socialise in the evening | The evenings everyone else is free. Birthdays become "come round at 19:00" | Real sleep on every off day, and a first shift that isn't a crisis |
| **Flip** | Move sleep back to night time across the off days, then move it forward again before the block | The first and last off day, burned on being mediocre at everything | Normal-family-time days off, at the price of two bad nights at each end |

Half the misery of days off comes from **starting the flip and not finishing it** — sleeping until 14:00 on day one, being up until 03:00 that night, then trying to sleep at 23:00 on day two when your body clock hasn't moved an hour. Decide at the start of the block, in pencil, on the roster.

## The one number that decides everything

A clock can be moved by roughly **1 to 1.5 hours per day** of deliberate light and timing, and moving it backwards costs about twice as much time as moving it forwards. So:

- **2 days off** — you cannot flip. Two days buys 2 to 3 hours of movement, which isn't a schedule, it's a jet lag you didn't need. **Hold.** Protect the morning sleep, put one thing you enjoy at 19:00, and be in bed by the usual time.
- **3 days off** — hold the first, flip across the second and third if you actually have something on them worth the price. If you don't, hold all three. That's the honest calculation: two nights of rubbish sleep is a real cost, not a technicality.
- **4 or 5 days off** — flip, with the bridge day doing the work. This is where a full reset is achievable, and where the plan below applies.
- **A week or more off** — flip fully. You'll want your life back, and the clock has time to arrive. Treat the return as a fresh 4-day ramp, not as "back to normal on Monday".

If you're on a rotation with an irregular number of off days, use the *shortest* gap in the pattern to set the default, and let the long gaps be the luxury ones.

## The bridge day, hour by hour

The bridge day is the first day off after the last shift. Its only job is to start the move without wrecking either the sleep you need or the sleep you'll need tomorrow. Done properly it looks like a bad day that works; done properly twice in a row, it's a habit.

| When | Do | Why this and not the obvious thing |
|---|---|---|
| Off shift, morning | Sleep in a dark room until a **fixed** time — no more than 2 hours past your usual wake | Sleeping 9 hours "to catch up" moves the clock in the wrong direction and steals tonight's sleepiness. Recovery sleep is real; the catch-up version isn't |
| On waking | 10 to 20 minutes of bright light, ideally outside, plus food within the hour | Light on waking and a meal at the same time each day are the two levers that say *this is the start*. Both, on the first morning, are worth more than the nap you'll want at 15:00 |
| Early afternoon | One 90-minute nap maximum, before 15:00, alarm set | A 90-minute block doesn't leave you in deep sleep at the alarm. After 15:00 it's a loan against tonight |
| Evening | Stay up to your **target night bedtime**, 22:30–23:30 for most people. Nothing strenuous, nothing that needs a brain | The first night in a bed at night is the one that fails if you're not sleepy. Tired-and-bored is the goal, not wired |
| Night 2, 3 | Fixed wake time every single day, no exceptions, morning light every day | The anchor is the wake time, not the bedtime — bedtime is what you're trying to move, so it can't also be what you hold onto |
| Last off day | Bed by the usual night-shift-eve time, 20-minute nap at 16:00 if you need it | This is the day the block gets lost, because it's the last free evening. The nap on the last off day is not optional if you have a drive in front of you |

The two mistakes that eat a bridge day are chores and optimism. Errands, admin, the DIY that's been waiting — all of it goes **after 16:00**, because the morning is the treatment, not the waiting room. And optimism is the belief that you'll be fine for the birthday lunch at 13:00 on the second off day; you will be fine at the lunch and wrecked at 21:00, which is the hour your sleep is being built.

## 2-2-3 (the rota with no room for a bridge day)

Two days, two off, three on, alternating days and nights. The off day between two blocks of nights is one day long, which means there's nothing to build — you hold, and you plan the *one* thing you get.

| Day | Pattern | Sleep | The one decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Nights | 08:30–14:00 | Anchor everything, including on the day you'd like to sleep more |
| 3 | **The single day off** | 08:30–13:00, then up | One nap max 90 min in the late afternoon if there's an evening commitment; nothing before 16:00 counts as rest, it counts as the plan |
| 4–5 | Days | 23:00–06:30, moved in 2 steps | 2 hours per day is the ceiling. Don't try to arrive in one jump |
| 6–8 | Nights | 08:30–14:00 | The first night back is the bad one; put the light and the nap in regardless of how you feel |
| 9 | Off (next block starts) | — | Repeat. The pattern is the asset, not any individual night |

The trap in a 2-2-3 is day 3: a single off day between two rotations feels like it must be *for something*, so it gets spent on the washing, the shop and the family, and neither sleep nor the social thing gets done. Choose in advance which of the two the day is for. Both is the answer that produces the bad month.

On 5-on-3-off nights (five nights then three off), the third off day is your flip day and the first is a bridge day — which is exactly the eight-day shape in [4 on, 4 off](/4-on-4-off-sleep/), with the same two handovers and less room in the middle. The tables there are the same arithmetic with different labels; what changes is that the last of your three off days must be protected, because it's the one that decides whether night one is survivable.

## What makes the flip actually work, and it isn't a gadget

Two things, both free, both about light:

1. **Morning light on the days you want to be a day person.** Outside beats a window, ten to twenty minutes, every day of the flip, wake time fixed at the target hour even when the night before was poor. A bad night plus the fixed wake time produces a good day; a bad night plus sleeping until 11:00 produces the schedule collapse that costs you the next two days.
2. **Sunglasses on the drive home from the last night shift, and dim from the moment you're back indoors.** The morning after the last shift is the one high-light day that pushes you the wrong way, so the sunglasses aren't a comfort item; they're the first hour of treatment for the flip.

Then the two things that quietly break the plan are worth naming, because they're not sleep problems, they're calendar problems: the **family's Saturday evening** (schedule the flip so the good evening lands on a day you'll be awake for, rather than the one you're recovering on) and **the drive** (a long Sunday-evening drive after a flip day is the worst time-of-day combination a shift worker can put themselves in). If your flip ends with a 90-minute motorway journey at the point where your clock says 03:00, that's the one thing to re-plan.

If you'd rather not re-plan a drive: don't flip. The hold strategy is not the lesser option; for a lot of two-day gaps it's the correct one, and it's the reason this page starts with a decision instead of a tip list.

One rule outranks the whole page, and it's the one that catches people on the last off day rather than the first: **never drive when you're drowsy.** The drive home off the final night, and the Sunday-evening drive at the end of a flip, are the two windows where a night-shift body is worst and the least able to notice. You cannot self-assess drowsiness reliably, because the part of the brain that would notice is the part that's asleep. If you realise you're drifting, the only useful sequence is: stop somewhere safe, 200 mg of caffeine, 20 minutes asleep, then move slowly or not at all — that buys you about half an hour of margin, which is enough to get home and nowhere near enough to make a long motorway run in the middle of your biological night.

**Stop optimising and get help if:** you cannot sleep at night on *either* schedule for weeks and it isn't the noise and light in your bedroom; you're dozing unintentionally at any point in the block, including behind the wheel; your mood is low through the whole off period rather than merely tired; or the rota itself is the problem — a block that gets longer every month is a roster conversation, not a personal sleep failure. And if you're considering anything at all to shift your timing, that's a pharmacist or clinician conversation with your own medication list in hand; this site gives no dosing plans for anything, deliberately.
