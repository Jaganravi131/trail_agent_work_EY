# Editorial policy

Short, because a policy nobody can read in two minutes is a policy written for the footer.

## 1. This is educational content, not medical advice

Nothing on this site diagnoses, treats, prevents or cures anything, including shift work sleep disorder, insomnia, apnoea, or anything involving a medication. Dosing plans for prescription drugs are a conversation with a prescriber. Dosing plans for supplements are a conversation with a pharmacist or clinician — this site names none, on purpose, because a number lifted from a paper and applied to your own physiology is how people get hurt.

Practical consequence: every article ends with the same list of "stop self-optimising and get this looked at" flags, and the safety rule about never driving drowsy appears wherever it is relevant, even when it makes the page less pleasant to read.

## 2. Credentials, stated plainly

The author is a pen name for a person with lived experience of night work and **no clinical qualification**. No article, page, book blurb or social post will imply otherwise. No white coats, no stethoscope stock photography, no "as a sleep expert" phrasing, and no use of the word "clinic" anywhere it could be mistaken for a service being offered.

This is not modesty, it's arithmetic: a health site that implies credentials it does not have can be removed by the platform, refused by the storefront and reported to a regulator. One sentence of honesty is cheaper than that.

## 3. Money, disclosed

- If a link here goes to a retailer and pays a commission, it is labelled **immediately** at the link, in words, not in a tooltip. Affiliate disclosure is a legal requirement in the US (FTC) and expected everywhere.
- **No paid placements inside editorial text.** A product is never recommended because a brand asked. If a brand ever pays for anything on this site, the paid surface is labelled and kept out of the articles.
- **No sponsored reviews, no gifted-unit swaps, no review rings.** Never ask a reader for a positive review; never ask for a review at all in exchange for anything, including a discount or a free PDF. Asking an honest stranger for an honest opinion is fine — attaching a condition to it is not.
- No income claims about this site itself. Nobody reading a page here should be told they can earn something by doing what I did.

## 4. Sourcing standard

Physiological claims name their source: a named organisation (sleep foundation, CDC/NIOSH guidance on shift work, occupational-safety authority) or a named study, linked from the page. Where a number is a rule of thumb rather than a finding, the text says so. Where the evidence is genuinely contested — light timing being the obvious one — the page states the disagreement instead of picking the tidy answer.

Every page carries a `last reviewed` date and the underlying claim set is re-checked on a schedule, not "when someone complains".

## 5. Originality

All of it is written here. Nothing is spun from a competitor's article, paraphrased from a copyrighted book, or copied from a press release. No product review is generated from other people's reviews; that practice breaks retailer terms and, for some data classes, worse.

## 6. Corrections

Reported errors get checked against the cited source. If we were wrong, the text changes and a line appears here:

| Date | Page | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-13 | — | policy published; no corrections logged yet |

A correction with no date is an apology note nobody can audit. A correction table with nothing in it is a table that should stay mostly empty and never hidden.

## 7. What we won't do, in one list

- No fabricated testimonials, and no invented credentials — not in a bio, not in a byline, not in a caption.
- No scraping of storefronts, forums or review corpora to build content, and no buying, swapping or bartering engagement of any kind.
- No undisclosed AI-written content: where a platform requires disclosure, disclose. It costs nothing here and costs the account elsewhere.
- No health claims that need a licence to make.
- No page that trades on a reader being too tired to check it.

*Questions about any of the above belong in the same place as corrections: the contact address in the footer. If a claim on this site cannot be defended with a named source, it gets removed rather than argued.*
