# About

This site covers sleep, food and caffeine for people who work overnight: nurses, paramedics, warehouse and plant crews, dispatch, security, support engineers on call rotation, and anyone whose employer decided 23:00–07:00 is a reasonable shift.

## Who writes it

One person, under the pen name **J. Arek Thorne**. I have spent years around night work — not as a clinician. **I am not a doctor, not a nurse, not a sleep specialist, and I hold no clinical licence of any kind.** The bio on the book's back page says the same thing, deliberately, because the version of this sentence that claims credentials is the version that gets a health site taken down and the author in front of a regulator.

What I do have is the systems half: enough time watching handovers go wrong to know that the failure is usually timing, food and light, not willpower. And a habit of writing the arithmetic down instead of the advice.

## What the site is

- **Practical scheduling math.** Every article gives times, doses and the mechanism behind them, with the limits stated. Where a number is a rule of thumb, the page says so.
- **A free tool.** [The planner](/planner/) is a single HTML file with no sign-up, no accounts, no tracking and no network calls — it computes your nap, caffeine cutoff and light times from your shift start and finish. It works offline. That's not a giveaway, it's the point: a tool you can bookmark is more useful to you than a gated PDF, and it is the reason the rest of the site can stay free.
- **One paid thing.** A book, *The Night Shift Reset*, which is the 28-day version of the same method with logs and the days-off strategy. The articles do not hold back the useful part to sell it — the book goes deeper and adds structure you can't get from three pages.

## What it is not

Not medical advice, not a diagnosis, not a treatment plan, and not occupational-safety guidance. If your symptoms or your roster are the problem, the answer is a clinician or your employer's safety process, not a page here. Full terms on the [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) page.

Two commitments worth putting in writing:

1. **No fake expertise.** No white coat in the header, no invented credentials in the author box, no "doctors recommend" that isn't sourced to a named study.
2. **No fabricated experience or reviews.** No testimonials were written here, and none were bought. If reviews exist on a storefront, they came from readers without prompting, incentive or swap.

Send an error to the contact address in the site footer. *(Operator note: add the address when the site deploys — this repo ships the copy, not an inbox.)* If it is a factual mistake, it gets fixed and logged on the [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) page with the date and what changed — including for the book, in its next file revision.

*Everything here is original writing. Nothing was scraped from a competitor's listing, a review corpus or a book, and no claim on this site depends on a reader being too tired to check it.*
