## A necessary disclaimer

This book is educational. It is not medical advice, not a diagnosis, and not a treatment for any condition. It does not treat, cure or prevent disease. It contains no supplement or drug recommendations presented as therapy.

Shift work interacts with real physiology. If you have — or suspect you have — a sleep disorder, sleep apnoea (snoring plus waking unrefreshed is the classic tell, and it is rampant and undertreated in shift populations), depression, a cardiac condition, diabetes, or a gastrointestinal diagnosis, talk to a clinician before changing anything. If you take any prescription medication, ask your pharmacist specifically about timing: several common drugs have circadian-dependent absorption or interaction profiles, and a pharmacist will answer that for free in ninety seconds.

Two hard safety rules override everything in these pages:

**Never drive when you are drowsy.** No technique in this book is worth a crash. Chapter 7 offers the alternatives, including ones that cost nothing.

**Never change a safety-critical work practice** — medication administration, machinery operation, security posture, overnight monitoring — in a way that conflicts with your employer's protocol or your professional standard of care. If a suggestion here clashes with your employer's rules, your employer's rules win and you should tell me about it in a review, because I'd want to know.

Eating more protein and going to bed at a consistent time is not dangerous for a healthy adult. Chasing a "reset" until you collapse at the wheel of a car at 7:40 a.m. is. You are the operator of your own risk.

## How to use this book

Read chapter 1. Then set five numbers and start tonight: chapters 2 through 6 each hand you one. Ignore the rest. Run the 28-day protocol in chapter 8 with the log in chapter 9, and add the other material as you need it.

Do not read this book end to end at 4 a.m. trying to fix your entire life in one night. That is how most people fail at this, and I have done it myself: a Sunday-evening download of eleven PDFs, a plan with nineteen rules, gone by Wednesday.

Here is the compressed version, so you can start before you finish chapter 1.

### The five numbers

| # | Number | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **3-2-1-0** | Hours before sleep: last food 3, last caffeine 2 or ideally 6, lights down 1, zero clock-watching at 0. |
| 2 | **30 g** | Protein at your anchor meal, the one meal you eat at the same clock time every shift. |
| 3 | **400 mg / 200 mg** | Caffeine ceiling per day and per dose. Not a target — a ceiling. |
| 4 | **10 min** | Bright light in your first hour on shift. The cheapest alertness tool you have, and it's free. |
| 5 | **4.5 + 1.5** | Core sleep plus anchor nap, in hours. Six hours of planned sleep beats eight hours of lying in the dark doing arithmetic. |

And one sequence, which matters more than all five:

> **Descent → Hold → Reentry.**
> Your shift is not twelve hours of suffering. It is three short problems with different solutions. Most advice fails because it treats them as one.

Everything in this book is an elaboration of that line.

### What this is not

This is not a book about moral failing. If you have eaten vending-machine crackers at 3:30 a.m. for four years and told yourself the problem is your discipline, the problem is not your discipline. The problem is that you were handed a body that expects daylight and a roster that doesn't care, and then given generic advice from someone who sleeps in a bed at 10:30 p.m. in a house where nothing happens at 6 a.m.

You don't need more willpower. You need defaults that survive being exhausted. That's what's here.

### A note on evidence

This is written from years around overnight work plus public guidance from sleep and occupational-health research that I've turned into decisions you can make in a break room. Where something is a firm population-level finding — caffeine's half-life, the roughly 90-minute structure of sleep cycles, the 400 mg/day figure that food regulators commonly cite for healthy adults — it's stated plainly. Where it's a rule of thumb I built because it worked and nobody had tested it, I say so, and chapter 8 hands you the means to test it on yourself in a month. Trust the calibrated version over the generic one. Including mine.
