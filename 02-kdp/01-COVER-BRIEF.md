# Cover Brief — The Night Shift Reset

Everything a designer (or you, in Canva Free) needs. No creative judgment left open.

| Asset | Size | Notes |

## Concept: "4:07 a.m., and the body thinks it's midnight"

The cover must read at 100 px wide in an Amazon result row. One idea. Big.

**Winning direction (build this one):**
A near-black navy field (#0B1220) filling the whole front. In the lower third, a single horizontal band of warm amber light (#F2A33C, gradient to transparent upward, soft 40% blur) — the light under a door, or the glow of a break-room vending machine. Nothing else in the artwork. The amber band must not touch the left or right edge — leave 0.35" of navy breathing room.

Over that: title in **all-caps condensed sans** (Barlow Condensed Bold, or Oswald Bold, or Archivo Black if condensed isn't available in Canva Free). Off-white #F4F1EA, not pure white — pure white looks cheap and glows against black on e-ink thumbnails. Letter-spacing +2% on the title.

**Layout, top to bottom (percent of cover height):**
- 0–7%: keep clear. Amazon overlays nothing here but Kindle storefront crops do.
- 9%: kicker line, small caps, 8% of cover width from left. Text: `THE 28-DAY SYSTEM`. Amber #F2A33C. Weight: medium. This is the differentiator — do not delete it.
- 16–46%: TITLE, three stacked lines, left-aligned at 8% margin:
  `NIGHT`
  `SHIFT`
  `RESET`
  Each line flush left at the same x. The block is deliberately taller than it is wide.
- 50%: thin rule, 1.5 pt, #F4F1EA at 30% opacity, from left margin to 78% of width.
- 53–69%: subtitle, 2 lines, left-aligned, #C8CEDA, weight regular, size 40% of title:
  `Eating, caffeine and light timing for nurses, EMTs, warehouse and overnight desk crews`
  `who are tired at 10 a.m. and wired at 3 p.m.`
  Note the subtitle is written for **search**, not poetry. It contains the audience nouns.
- 70–82%: the amber light band lives here. Title text sits above it.
- 88–95%: author name, left margin, letterspaced +6%, #F4F1EA, 55% of title size: `J. AREK THORNE`
- Bottom right, 92%: tiny badge, 1 pt outline rounded rect, text `includes printable planner + free web tool`. This line is the value argument. Amber stroke, amber text.

**Back cover (paperback):**
Navy continues. Top 10% margin. One line in amber, 14 pt caps: `You can't fix a night shift with willpower.` Then body copy, 11 pt, #C8CEDA, 26–28 lines, left aligned, 12% side margins:

> Nobody tells you what to actually do at 3 a.m.
>
> Not what to eat, not when to stop the coffee, not how to sleep
> through a Saturday morning that your neighbours have filled with
> a lawnmower. The advice you get is generic: darken the room,
> drink water, go to bed at the same time. Same time as who? Your
> body doesn't own a clock that matches your roster.
>
> The Night Shift Reset replaces guesswork with five numbers and one
> sequence you can run on a 12-hour rotation, a 4-on-4-off pattern,
> or nights-only. You get the anchor-meal rule, the caffeine curfew
> math, a light curtain you can build from a towel and foil, a split
> sleep architecture that stops the 90-minute lie in bed, a bridge-day
> plan for days off, and a script for asking your scheduler for the
> rotation that would actually help.
>
> Then 28 days of calibration, so the system ends up tuned to your
> body rather than a study you'll never read.

Below that: three bullet "who this is for" lines, a barcode zone (KDP places it — reserve 2"×1.2" bottom-right, keep 0.25" clear of any text), and the author line.

**Spine (6"×9", ~170 pages ≈ 0.40"):** navy. Title stacked or rotated 90°, off-white, 10 pt. Author last name at foot. No tagline.

## Colour psychology, stated so you can defend it if you deviate

Navy = night, authority, low-arousal. Amber = the low-blue end of the spectrum, the part a body clock reacts to least; it also reads as "warm interior / safety" against cold dark. Off-white type on navy tests better than white for readability at thumbnail scale because it lowers glare. Deliberately excluded: bright teal gradients, white coats, clip-art moons, cartoon nurses, photos of smiling people sleeping. Those signal "clinic pamphlet" or "AI template." Any stock photo of a nurse in a hospital must be commercially licensed for print; if you can't verify the license, use the abstract version above. That is why the recommended cover has **zero photography** — it removes the entire licensing risk and still looks intentional.

## Typography licenses
Barlow, Archivo, Oswald, Lato: OFL, free for commercial use including print covers, no attribution required. Download from Google Fonts. Do not use Helvetica/Arial system exports from a random template whose base file is licensed to someone else.

## What to test before committing
Render three 300 px wide JPEGs: this cover, one with photo of empty break room at night, one with type-only on amber field. Look at them inside an actual Amazon search results screenshot for "shift work sleep". The one you can read while squinting at arm's length wins. Historically that is the navy/amber type-forward version because photo covers lose the title to the thumbnail crop.
